Interview prep tracker

A study checklist for system design and GenAI interviews that you tick off, grade and write notes on. It remembers your progress in this browser, tells you what to revise and when, and opens the lesson on this site for each topic that has one.

The tracker

      How it works

      1. Pick a sheet. System design covers the general backend and distributed-systems topics, GenAI goes deeper on retrieval, agents, serving and evaluation, the interview questions are the ones to answer aloud, and design practice lists the classic design problems with the steps of a good answer.
      2. Tick an item when you could explain it aloud or build it. Mark it in progress while you study, completed when you are done, and needs revision when you have studied it but could not explain it with confidence.
      3. Grade your confidence from 1 to 5. 1 means you cannot explain it, 3 that you can explain the basics, and 5 that you could teach it and answer the follow-ups.
      4. Come back when it is due. The next review comes 1, 3, 7, 14 or 30 days after the day you studied an item, for confidence 1 to 5. The “to revise” button shows what is due today.
      5. Write notes. Open a row to write what you understood and what to practise next. A search looks in your notes too.

      Where your progress lives

      Everything you tick or write is saved in this browser, on this device. You do not need an account, and nothing is sent to a server unless you choose to sync. That keeps it private and instant, and it means the progress does not follow you to another browser by itself. Use Export a backup to save a file you can keep, load it again with Import a backup, or move it to another browser. A sheet can also be exported as a CSV for a spreadsheet. Clearing this site’s data in your browser clears the tracker, so export a backup first, or sync.

      Syncing across devices is optional. If you sign in with Google you can turn it on under the tracker: a copy of your progress, notes included, is then kept on this site’s server for your account, so the same progress is there on every device you turn it on in. Only you can read it. The most recent change to a row wins, so a tick on your phone and a note on your laptop are both kept. Stop syncing deletes the copy on the server (your browser keeps its own), and deleting your account deletes it too.

      The lessons behind the items

      Open an item to see the lessons on this site that cover it, each with the course it belongs to; a topic that has a video also has an Open the lesson link beside its title. A step of a design problem lists lessons for the problem and for the step, such as requirements, capacity estimation or the data model, and under them are books, documentation and papers to read further. For the networking items, the Network Lab has interactive tools, and the Practice Center has quizzes, coding problems and mock interviews for checking yourself afterwards.
