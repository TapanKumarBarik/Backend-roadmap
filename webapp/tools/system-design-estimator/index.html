System Design Sizing Rules of Thumb

1. The 86,400 Seconds Rule

A standard day has 24 × 3600 = 86,400 seconds. In high-pressure interviews, round up to 100,000 seconds for lightning-fast mental math. For example, 10 million daily requests divided by 100k seconds equals approximately 100 QPS.

2. Peak vs. Average Traffic ($2\times$ to $5\times$)

User traffic is never evenly distributed. Daily diurnal curves, timezone bursts, and notification pushes create sharp peaks. System engineers typically size infrastructure for $2.5\times$ to $3\times$ average QPS, and up to $5\times$ for flash sales or breaking news.

3. The Pareto 80/20 Memory Cache Rule

In almost all consumer applications, 20% of content generates 80% of read traffic. Sizing your Redis or Memcached cluster to hold 20% of daily read volume guarantees high cache hit rates (>90%), shielding primary databases from read overload.

4. Disk Capacity vs. IOPS Bottlenecks

Sharding calculations must evaluate both volume (e.g. 2 TB NVMe capacity per shard) and throughput (e.g. 25,000 IOPS per SSD). High write loads may necessitate sharding long before disk space is exhausted.
